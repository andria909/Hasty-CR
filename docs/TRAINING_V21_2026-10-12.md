# v21: lane defence, earlier Hog, spell lead

The user stopped live play after two completed matches and asked to train the
six tactical changes instead. The interrupted third match is not a result.
The completed v20 matches were both losses; Hog was used only 2 and 3 times.

The live and simulator safety masks now score pressure by lane, prioritising
building-targeting pushes over an isolated nearer unit. Hog can be offered at
6 elixir (7 with a crown lead), retaining at least two elixir for defence; it
no longer waits unconditionally for a full bar. A surviving Knight, Musketeer,
or Electro Spirit can lead a same-lane Hog counterpush. Defenders are still
held on an empty board, and active pressure still blocks Hog. On live vision,
spell aiming forecasts another 750 ms beyond the 500 ms capture-to-tap delay.
The simulator already resolves projectiles at their actual delayed impact and
uses spell hit/miss and prediction rewards, so this is not a separate flat
750 ms simulator delay.

The v21 PPO run started from v20, used a 0.03 anchor, 1e-5 learning rate,
four CPU environments, 50/50 rule-engine/meta opponents, and ran 60,160 steps.
The 30,208-step checkpoint was selected by the worst of the two small
intermediate evaluations. The final checkpoint scored lower and was not
promoted.

## Paired held-out evaluation

Both checkpoints used the new strategic mask and identical seed 986000,
80 matches per opponent family.

| Model | Rule-engine wins | Meta-deck wins | Hog share, rule | Hog share, meta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v20 weights | 59/80 (73.8%) | 30/80 (37.5%) | 14% | 15% |
| v21 weights | 69/80 (86.2%) | 47/80 (58.8%) | 10% | 12% |

The result favours v21 in simulation, but Hog frequency *fell* compared with
v20 under the same mask. Do not claim that the attack-cadence problem is
solved. Live play may differ because unit HP, movement, and enemy elixir are
estimated from vision rather than observed perfectly. Test v21 in a short
live series before calling it the new live default.
